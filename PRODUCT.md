# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Regulatory bodies and government enforcement agencies auditing ISP compliance with court-ordered or regulatory DNS takedown orders. Users are auditors and compliance officers — not developers. They open the tool, run a scan or review results, read a table, and export evidence. Context: formal work environment, likely during an audit cycle. They need to trust what they see.

## Product Purpose

Verifies that ISPs are correctly blocking access to court-ordered domains via DNS. A domain that fails to resolve = compliant (block is working). A domain that resolves = violation (ISP has not enforced the block). The tool captures screenshots of resolving (violating) sites as evidence. Success means an auditor can scan a list of domains, see which ISPs are compliant, and have evidence ready for a report.

## Brand Personality

Authoritative, precise, neutral. The interface speaks like a government audit report: plainly, without embellishment, without editorial. Data is presented as fact. The tool doesn't celebrate results or dramatize violations — it documents them.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards: cream or white backgrounds with blue accents, soft shadows, rounded stat cards (Mixpanel, HubSpot, generic AI dashboard style).
- Security product clichés: neon-on-black, animated threat maps, globe visualizations, "hacker aesthetic" styling.
- Generic 4-card horizontal metric rows at the top of every screen.
- Decorative borders used as structural elements or left-side accent stripes.
- Gradient backgrounds or gradient text.
- Any layout that looks like it was generated by an AI from a "SaaS dashboard" prompt.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence first.** The core value is documented proof of violations. Every screen should make compliance status and evidence immediately legible — no digging, no decoding.
2. **Authority through restraint.** Credibility comes from what is not there. No decoration that doesn't carry information. If removing an element wouldn't be missed, remove it.
3. **Neutral presentation.** The interface doesn't editorialize. A violation is a fact in a row, not a red alarm. The auditor draws conclusions; the tool presents data.
4. **Operational density.** Designed for people doing a job, not for demos. Prioritize scannable tables and clear actions over visual interest.
5. **Clarity at a glance.** An auditor returning to a scan result after two days should understand the state of compliance in under five seconds without re-reading the interface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG level required. Reasonable contrast ratios and keyboard navigation throughout. Color must never be the sole indicator of compliance status — pair color with text labels or icons.
